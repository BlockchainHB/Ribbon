# Ribbon copywriting research

22 October 2026. Research and approved direction for the agent-first website and README.

## Recommendation

Lead with agent-based work: keeping an agent next to the editor, canvas or preview where its output is reviewed. The brief names the audience; research supplies messaging principles, not evidence that this audience will convert.

Recommended draft: **Your agents, right beside your work.**

Support: Codex beside your IDE. Claude beside your canvas. Ribbon gives your Mac a scrolling workspace for everything you’re working on.

The broader alternative, “Your screen ends. Your workspace keeps going.”, explains scrolling memorably but does not identify the primary audience. Keep it as a secondary direction rather than the main promise.

## Diagnosis

“Your windows, side by side” describes an arrangement any ordinary tiler could show. The previous hero fit three generic windows on one screen; the first body section started with pairing. It gave no clear demonstration of how the scrolling strip helps when more work is open than fits comfortably on the display.

The problem is an undifferentiated promise and missing proof. A blanket ban on features would be the wrong correction. Features should explain why the outcome is credible.

## Message hierarchy

1. Audience and outcome: agents beside the work they help produce.
2. Mechanism: a scrolling workspace with useful window widths.
3. Jobs: agent + IDE; multiple separate agent windows; agent + design tool.
4. Broader relevance: everyday app pairs and independent displays.
5. Practical questions: requirements, permissions, scope and beta limitations.

The website should sell the working situation. The README should explain the tool and how to use it.

## Evidence

### Ogilvy

Source: [Ogilvy](https://gundir.com/resource/how-to-create-advertising-that-sells-by-david-ogilvy/). Original 1972 agency ad, reproduced by Gundir.

Choose one meaningful promise and demonstrate that the product delivers it.

Ribbon: Lead with keeping agents beside the work. Let scrolling, pairing and overview supply the evidence.

### Claude Hopkins

Source: [Claude Hopkins](https://www.scientificadvertising.com/ScientificAdvertising.pdf). Scientific Advertising (1923), public-domain text reproduction; chapters 3, 5 and 7.

Specific, believable statements carry more meaning than sweeping praise.

Ribbon: Say Codex beside your IDE, rather than effortless productivity or the ultimate workspace.

### Joanna Wiebe

Source: [Joanna Wiebe](https://copyhackers.com/2012/06/101-copywriting-dos-and-donts/). Direct author article; especially the features-versus-benefits section.

There is no universal rule that benefits must precede features. Write what the audience needs to understand.

Ribbon: Name the job first. Explain scrolling plainly so the promise has a credible mechanism.

### April Dunford

Source: [April Dunford](https://www.aprildunford.com/post/a-quickstart-guide-to-positioning). Direct author positioning guide.

Value depends on what someone would otherwise do and which differences they care about.

Ribbon: The working alternative is juggling overlapping agent, editor and browser windows. This audience hypothesis comes from the brief, not customer interviews.

### Eugene Schwartz

Source: [Eugene Schwartz](https://breakthroughadvertisingbook.com/the-five-states-of-awareness-the-key-to-marketing-that-actually-works/). Current publisher explanation of the awareness framework; not the original book text.

Match the message to what the visitor already understands.

Ribbon: A visitor may recognize juggling Claude and an editor before knowing the term scrolling window manager. Keep the category explanation directly below the promise.

### Apple’s iPod launch

Source: [Apple’s iPod launch](https://www.apple.com/newsroom/2001/10/23Apple-Presents-iPod/). Apple’s original launch announcement, 23 October 2001.

“1,000 songs in your pocket” turns storage capacity into a recognizable human outcome.

Ribbon: Translate window mechanics into a visible working situation. Preserve the mechanism as proof.

### Joanna Wiebe: customer language

Source: [Joanna Wiebe: customer language](https://copyhackers.com/voice-of-customer-in-your-brand/). Direct author workshop transcript.

Use the audience’s language rather than inventing a grand brand voice.

Ribbon: Use agents, IDE, canvas, review and windows. Avoid orchestrate your potential, unlock flow, and invented customer testimonials.

## Current product examples

- [Screen Studio](https://screen.studio/): “Beautiful screen recordings, in minutes.”. An immediately recognizable result, followed by a demonstration. The timing claim belongs to Screen Studio.
- [Raycast](https://www.raycast.com/): “Your shortcut to everything.”. A broad promise, grounded immediately in its launcher category and working examples.
- [CleanShot](https://cleanshot.com/): “Make your point, fast”. A task-level section heading connects capture tools to communication.
- [Chain Your Mac](https://chainyourmac.com/): “scrolls instead of shrinking”. A direct competitor explains the mechanism and its consequence. Scrolling itself cannot be presented as exclusive to Ribbon.
- [Codex](https://openai.com/index/introducing-the-codex-app/): “Work with multiple agents in parallel”. The launch describes directing work, reviewing changes and opening them in an editor. This supports the use case, not a Ribbon integration claim.
- [Claude Code](https://claude.com/product/claude-code): “Steer and review”. Its opening names concrete tasks and the places people review them. Ribbon can show the physical windows around that work.
- [Claude Design](https://claude.com/product/design): “Design your idea with Claude”. The page follows an idea through refinement and handoff. That makes agent-plus-canvas a useful scene rather than another generic window diagram.

## Proof and claim discipline

Show opening another window, moving through the strip and returning. Show paired neighbors moving together. Show the real overview with icons and titles. Names such as Codex, Claude and IDE describe examples of windows, not integrations or endorsements.

Avoid claims about running or coordinating agents, task status, faster AI, automatic context sharing, every agent session visible at once, never resizing, cross-restart pair persistence, or flawless multi-display behavior. The product does not justify those promises.

No customer interviews, conversion tests or fresh hardware compatibility tests were performed. These are defensible draft directions, not validated winners. Product examples are observations, not proof of conversion. Do not use historical advertising results as current performance estimates.




## Approved Paper revision

[Editable design](https://app.paper.design/file/01M5GQFBZ419QN9EYEE6N9TDS9/p-2-0) — Agent-first desktop and mobile artboards. The user approved the design for website and repository implementation.

Hero: Your agents, right beside your work.

Support: Codex beside your IDE. Claude beside your canvas. A scrolling workspace for everything you’re working on.

1. Keep the agent beside the code. Read the plan, review the changes, and check the result. Pair the windows you want to move together.
2. More agents. One workspace. Codex, Claude, another task. Find the window you need in the overview, then return to your work.
3. Keep the idea beside the canvas. Work through an idea with Claude while your design stays in view.
4. Your everyday work, too. Notes beside your browser. Mail beside your calendar. A separate workspace on each display.

Closing: Bring your workspace together. Download.

Paper QA: Hero spacing, readable type, contrast, alignment and intentional strip cropping reviewed. Coding scene clipped status removed. Overview labels/icons aligned on both widths. Design scene and regular-work scene reviewed. FAQ and closing keep the white surface. Complete desktop and mobile reviewed at their own widths; artboards use fit-content height. All scenes are illustrations, not hardware verification; Paper controls are static.


## Design rules

White surfaces and footer; Cobalt Fold identity; SF Pro medium headings; generous spacing. One heading and one sentence per section. No repeated eyebrows, settings-as-feature sections, tag cards, fabricated metrics or integration claims. Examples use actual application names: Codex, Claude, Terminal, Cursor and Paper.

See [the Tailark references](https://tailark.com/) for the layout study’s source library. The approved design follows its media-first structure, without copying licensed component source.
